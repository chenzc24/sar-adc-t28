How a SAR ADC was designed and reworked

8-bit asynchronous SAR ADC · design review

Several costly revisions began before layout. Two blocks held the same reference state, RC delays survived a digital-cell migration, and qualified blocks met their real pads only at integration. The circuit revisions, extractions and rule checks show where an earlier decision could have saved a rebuild.

Scope & resultsDesign iterationsLessons & next steps

Converter scope

The core runs at a nominal 0.90 V. Differential input spans −0.90 to +0.90 V around 0.45 V common mode, giving 7.031 mV/LSB. External SAMPLE allocates 45 ns to acquisition and 55 ns to conversion. Bootstrap switches sample onto the MOM CDAC, and eight asynchronous comparisons steer the reference switches.

The board supplies the input drive, reference voltages and sample clock. The chip includes reference switching, gate drive and decoupling. Reference generation, sample-clock generation, continuous-time analog input buffering and offset calibration are external. Digital results reach the pads through an on-chip register, Q buffers and output drivers.

Design decisions that had to be revisited

In October a simpler handshake produced all eight bits in the controller bench. In November the read deadline resurfaced in the RC macro, inside the library delay cell and at the board output. Each step added a load, clock path or observation point that the previous test did not exercise. The chronology below identifies the revision and test boundary at each decision.

August–15 November

Front-end convergence and its physical assumptions

The 10-bit behavioral model first tested the noise allocation in August. September experiments isolated bootstrap turn-off paths under CDAC load. October’s flat 8-bit array removed the split-capacitor bridge ratio, but the choice of unit capacitance changed the sampling load and required a new switch test. All 16,384 ideal 8-bit model outputs matched Python code for code. Injecting the allocated noise reduced ENOB from 7.80 to 7.33 bits. With 500 Ω at the reference and 1,800 Ω at the bottom plates, the macro-model could still return the correct code with 4.41 LSB of settling error. The need to check analog values at each decision was visible before layout. 8-bit migration ↗

  1. Late August–19 October · Replace the Bootstrap topology.

    Sizing, discharge and reset-order experiments left both hold error and core-device stress. The designer’s dated notes identify the replacement topology they supplied. It used twelve T28 1.8 V MOS devices, a 1 pF bootstrap capacitor and a local inverter. On the same 10-bit split CDAC, worst differential hold error across five TT inputs fell from 1.536 to 0.394 mV. The 8-bit flat array increased the load. The 0.5 µm switch then gave 12.59 mV error; increasing it to 1 µm brought that down to 0.126 mV. Each pass belonged to a particular load and corner.

    Designer’s notes ↗ · Same-load comparison ↗ · 8-bit retest ↗

  2. 20 October · Simplify the SAR before drawing it.

    The custom CMOS controller acknowledged decisions and resets, handled timeouts and retained a separate output bank. At slow-hot it finished seven bits by the deadline. SAMPLE then initialized a local bit loop. Removing the extra bank and EOC reduced the expanded MOS count from 1,742 to 1,476. Thirteen functional and timing tests passed with ideal analog interfaces. The record already warned that a stalled comparison could leave a partial code. Chip integration still had to assign an output capture edge and pad drive.

    Controller and test boundary ↗

  3. 20–23 October · Reference control crossed an ideal interface.

    R1 closed the loop using physical reference switches and ideal gate control. In R3 a frame completed eight comparisons yet returned 1 instead of 65. Replaying the same input history isolated a rejected timer callback in the ideal controller; the fix restored code 65. R4 then implemented native reference control, followed by an experiment moving staged reset into idle. The contemporary notes flagged the handoff gap between the digital and analog work.

    Frame 9 replay ↗ · Native reference control ↗ · Idle-reset comparison ↗

  4. 24–29 October · The worst stress moved between devices.

    At FF cold, quartering the series gate resistance improved plate settling from 2.217 to 2.166 ns but deepened undershoot from −56.94 to −75.61 mV. Another trial added 4.084 pF of MOM across 32 gate lines. Overshoot at the targeted node fell about 85%, and the worst stress moved to the MSB reference PMOS bulk–drain pair. The capacitor candidate was dropped. On 29 October a document review identified the 0.99 V line as the model’s bias warning threshold. An applicable transient lifetime envelope was still missing. In retrospect, the team needed an explicit criterion for retaining a change before iterating on peaks.

    Gate-resistance trial ↗ · Capacitor and tolerant-device trials ↗ · Threshold review ↗

  5. 27 October–4 November · Map the gates, carry the RC delays forward.

    The team reapplied the gate changes to the frozen L90/Mmain2 parent, isolating them from stress-diagnostic variants. The migration mapped inverters, NANDs, resettable DFFs, clock buffers and reference drive. Its contract explicitly retained RC/clear networks, the external SR latch load and reference resistors. Frozen B gained full-code and FFT regression coverage. Physical area and routed read time for the RC paths lay outside that review. Layout later measured about 845 µm² for the timing macro’s 231 MOM capacitors and ten resistors alone.

  6. 15 November · Remove duplicate state from the CDAC switch driver.

    Reference switching and stress experiments had accumulated eight muxes, eight DFFs, two-step capture, per-bit reset, non-overlap generation and long drivers. Tracing who owned the trial word put decisions back in the SAR. The reference side kept common initial-state selection and gate drive. With real switch loads all five corners returned 18/18 correct frames. A separate thin-oxide duration screen still found nine violations at FF cold and one at FF hot, with none in the simplified driver itself.

  7. 15 November · Close two schematic stress paths separately.

    Changing only the two MSB final drivers from D1 to D2 cut cumulative FF-cold comparator input stress from 166.1 to 47.7 ps. The eight remaining violations were inside standard DFFs. Isolating data feedback during asynchronous clear suggested an OR at each D input. The worst duration fell from 131.5 to 52.5 ps, clearing all eight violations in the 18-frame schematic test. Stage 6 later found FF-cold stress on a different extracted netlist. Each result belongs to its tested revision.

16–21 November · StrongARM layout

Comparator · three shared-diffusion rows

The early sym2 StrongARM occupied 7.96 × 12.46 µm. Packing it into three rows exposed terminal-mapping and unequal finger-width problems. V7 used individual 0.5 µm fingers, explicit schematic connectivity, shared diffusion and input escapes between rows. The footprint fell from 99.18 to 5.74 µm².

V3/v4 grouped transistors into multifinger PCells. The gate assignments and source/drain names became difficult to reconcile with the circuit. V6 restored one instance per schematic device but introduced a 0.5/1 µm diffusion step and an extra tail row. V7 reconciled compact placement with traceable terminals.

On the same ten TT points, maximum decision delay fell from 517.3 to 301.7 ps, while peak differential kickback rose from 52.18 to 59.63 µV. V8 added M2 supply pins and redundant vias. Tail-to-ground resistance fell from 38.7 to 29.4 Ω and maximum delay was 314.3 ps. An extra clock via on one side caused a wrong decision at +0.1 mV, so the symmetric version was retained.

16–18 November · parallel with SAR layout

CDAC · shortening TOP routes exposed a small-bit weight error

Opening and restoring B1 in a unit MOM / 1:2 pilot verified that LVS detected the fault. The first full array then required 16.736 mm of functional routing and 404.44 µm of TOP per side. On 18 November facing adjacent TOP rows and folding 65 switch pairs into 16 per side cut the differential footprint from 8,354.62 to 3,738.62 µm². Each TOP route fell to 213.27 µm. In matched TT prefill fixtures the sum of bit weights rose from 95.292% to 96.994% of schematic, but the worst gain-normalized bit residual grew from 0.049 to 0.129 LSB.

Utility fill raised the fixed TOP load from about 38.6 to about 141 fF per side. With array-owned fill, conductor-level extraction then located a second error. Coupling inside each MOM unit was uniform, but long M4 trunks overweighted the small bits, giving −0.35 LSB DNL at transitions such as 47→48. Reordering just the central bits by weight produced standalone transient DNL/INL of 0.123/0.084 LSB with 4.22% gain error. All ten codes matched in the integrated SS-cold run, yet one frame exceeded the 0.25 LSB sampling limit at 0.351 LSB. That result sent the issue back to the sampler.

18–21 November · extracted recheck on 24 November

From RC to library delays to per-bit single-edge timing

  1. Nov 18 · The cost of RC timing

    The routed SAR macro occupied 2,058 µm². Its 231 MOM capacitors used 513 µm² and ten resistors another 333 µm². The 17.13 µm QUIET resistor passed in schematic and missed the SS-cold read by 6.11 ns after routing. An 11 µm trial still missed by 1.25 ns. An 8.5 µm resistor finally left 2.59 ns. A useful freeze criterion would have budgeted drawn area and delay under routed load.

    Area and resistor trials ↗

  2. Nov 19 · Standard delays passed a schematic stress screen

    V3 replaced five RC paths with 52 DEL and 28 AND cells. Four of six words were wrong on the first trial. Receiver buffers and final-tap request gating restored all 18 words at each of five corners. In the FF-cold two-frame thin-oxide scan, failing pairs fell from two to zero and worst over-limit duration from 457.8 to 75.0 ps against 100 ps. The result qualified that netlist and those waveforms. Routing requires another scan.

    Five corners and two-frame SOA ↗

  3. Nov 19 · Internal cell wiring changed the deadline

    Removing the QB capacitor still left 13.51 ns of SS-cold schematic margin on the nine-stage candidate. After coupled extraction it completed six comparisons by 90 ns in frame zero. Restoring the capacitor still gave six. At fixed slew and load, one DEL250 took 280 ps in schematic, 283 ps with device geometry alone and 471 ps including cell wiring. A later single-edge high tap measured 1.431 ns at FF-cold/CBEST_T and 3.894 ns at SS-cold/CWORST_T. Both fast-corner settling and slow-corner readout needed a budget.

    Capacitor restoration control ↗ · Paired tap delays ↗

  4. Nov 19–20 · Recovering read margin exposed late preset

    With a 40 ns sample pulse, shortening QUIET and sample enable let a dual-extracted DEL200 candidate read all four words. Yet its weighted error over 510 bottom plates reached 25.23 LSB at track opening in SS cold against 0.25 LSB. The team paused delay sweeps to review frame boundaries. The new single COMMIT edge stored the decision and advanced the trial, with preset on completion and the 29-stage idle guard removed. Under matched four-frame conditions, margin from last update to read rose from 3.642 to 10.917 ns.

    Track-opening error and stop decision ↗ · Matched replay ↗

  5. Nov 20 · A correct code hid the bit-6 reference error

    The initial 8/6 taps were checked against the instantaneous reference pins at all 510 bottom plates. A long 24/24-tap reference exposed −0.393 LSB of bit-6 TOP error at FF cold, including −0.355 LSB from reference recovery. A longer 9/6 tap then produced three bit-3 threshold crossings. Registering the select one commit earlier reduced them to one on the same 63→64 replay. Qualification now examined TOP, the reference pins and handoff edges at each decision.

    Long-window decomposition ↗ · Registered-select replay ↗

  6. Nov 20–21 · A 45 ns acquisition window and a bitwise budget

    Early single-edge trials still used 40/60 ns. Adoption moved the falling edge 5 ns later, allocating 45 ns to tracking and 55 ns to conversion while retaining the 90 ns read. Cell LPE and extracted-CDAC settling set the bit-6 through bit-0 taps to 11/9/7/6/5/4/4. SS-cold worst-interconnect margin was 7.59 ns with ideal SAR inter-cell wires. The routed SAR and extracted CDAC then passed 8/8 frames with 6.95 ns. Duty cycle, actual gate edges, per-bit waits and the external read aperture belong in a single timing budget.

  7. Nov 20 → 24 · Wider extraction reopened terminal stress

    Five FF-cold violations appeared before single-edge adoption. Changing the write-AND pin assignment and the select/reference logic brought the adopted 1,902-device scan to zero, with numerical resolution still under review. On the fill5 core with the supply/package network, the FF-cold extreme case found 16 over-duration VDS pairs, worst 757.8 ps. The netlists, loads, stimuli and scan scopes differ. 21–24 November · from core to package boundary

How IO and rule decisions propagated into fill

  1. Nov 21–22 · Blocks froze before pad supplies were settled.

    The first 7 × 7 plan had one analog PVDD3AC where the library required at least three, and omitted PVSS2AC. Checking supplies, corner cells and domain boundaries yielded a minimum of 34 pads. The 36-pad replacement changed placement, reference routing and the rail budget.

    Pad-rule audit ↗ · Revised rail budget ↗

  2. Nov 22 · The IO generator inserted an extra cut at each domain boundary.

    The plan called for one PRCUTA_G per boundary. The generator filled each 40 µm gap with two. Renaming supplies reduced ring ERC from 334 findings to six. The two floating wells and four floating substrates lay exactly at the abutting cuts. The ring review accepted the group because the findings were inside library cells. R8 replaced one cut at each boundary with PFILLER20_G. Neither cell contains devices, so the LVS netlist was insensitive to the mistake.

    Original ring disposition ↗ · R8 correction ↗

  3. Oct 20–Nov 22 · Who holds the previous conversion?

    The October SAR simplification removed the output bank. Sampling cleared the working word, so a correct core read did not establish a held board output. The first IO plan reserved a register outside the core, but the partial assembly passed its scoped LVS while the register was still undrawn. Eight DVDD flip-flops eventually captured on the same SAMPLE edge that cleared the core. Adding DEL250 on D raised FF-hot hold slack from 0.30 to 0.50 ns.

  4. Nov 22–23 · Connecting VIN to a pad reopened the frozen Bootstrap.

    The latch-up rules depended on pad connectivity. Hierarchical DRC reported only the negative side. A flat run found one plus six markers on each sampler. R2 widened the guard rings and moved the PMOS row to give 2.40 µm VIN-to-PMOS diffusion spacing against 2.30 µm required. The first widening ran into dummy poly and had to turn outward. Clearing latch-up reopened fill, extraction, standalone sampling and closed-loop qualification.

Where review should have intervened

The reference-driver issue identified duplicate state on 15 November. The floorplan issue called out scattered area and interface constraints on the 16th. The block audit catalogued missing XREF, top-level and standalone checks on the 21st. Several risks had already been raised. The handoff failed to carry each open condition to the next freeze. Partial LVS could correctly match a netlist without the output register. A name-dependent ERC probe could explain warnings without verifying the planned cut count. Both decisions needed a reviewer to check what was being accepted.

The design handoff needed to identify omitted loads and delays. Review needed an independent rule check and an end-to-end interface. Release needed a named owner for each exception. The records locate failures in those decisions. They provide insufficient evidence to assign individual negligence.

Lessons and next steps

  1. Resolve two measured excursions. At FF cold, 16 VDS pairs exceeded the duration rule, with a worst case of 757.8 ps against 100 ps. At FF hot, the sampler reached 4.130 mV single-ended hold error against 3.516 mV. Recheck the worst register and the sampling edge, including timing and differential-error consequences.
  2. Complete performance characterization. Complete the 1,024-point noisy FFT for SNDR and ENOB. Use its code histogram for an initial linearity estimate, report uncovered codes, and schedule full transition characterization separately. Translate the extracted comparator offset σ of 12.05 mV over 200 seeds into off-chip correction range and input headroom.
  3. Close physical and package handoff. Review the IO/ESD waivers and exclusion-zone vias, and reconcile final VIN/VREF parasitics. Confirm the 892 µm outline, the 36-pad bond map and board reference and clock conditions.
