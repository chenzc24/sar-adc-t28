From comparator to chip

8-bit asynchronous SAR ADC · design review

The filled core completed 12 short conversion tests across three corners. The chip includes pads, an output register, ESD clamps and a seal ring. The sampler has little margin and fast-corner stress tests identified devices that need attention.

Converter scope

The core runs at a nominal 0.90 V. Differential input spans −0.90 to +0.90 V around 0.45 V common mode, giving 7.031 mV/LSB. External SAMPLE allocates 45 ns to acquisition and 55 ns to conversion. Bootstrap switches sample onto the MOM CDAC, and eight asynchronous comparisons steer the reference switches.

The board supplies the input drive, reference voltages and sample clock. The chip includes reference switching, gate drive and decoupling. Reference generation, sample-clock generation, continuous-time analog input buffering and offset calibration are external. Digital results reach the pads through an on-chip register, Q buffers and output drivers.

Where layout changed the design

Comparator · three shared-diffusion rows

The early sym2 StrongARM occupied 7.96 × 12.46 µm. Packing it into three rows exposed terminal-mapping and unequal finger-width problems. V7 used individual 0.5 µm fingers, explicit schematic connectivity, shared diffusion and input escapes between rows. The footprint fell from 99.18 to 5.74 µm².

On the same ten TT points, maximum decision delay fell from 517.3 to 301.7 ps, while peak differential kickback rose from 52.18 to 59.63 µV. V8 added M2 supply pins and redundant vias. Tail-to-ground resistance fell from 38.7 to 29.4 Ω and maximum delay was 314.3 ps. An extra clock via on one side caused a wrong decision at +0.1 mV, so the symmetric version was retained.

SAR · simplify the states, then rebuild the timing

  1. Remove a handshake before layout. The custom controller acknowledged decisions and resets, handled timeouts and kept a separate output bank. At a slow corner it finished seven bits. SAMPLE then initialized a local compare/write/advance/settle loop. Removing the extra output bank and EOC reduced the expanded MOS count from 1,742 to 1,476. Thirteen bounded controller tests passed.
  2. RC delays consumed area and missed the read. The 231 MOM capacitors occupied 512.64 µm² and ten resistors another 332.57 µm². The schematic passed with a 17.13 µm QUIET resistor. After routing it missed the SS-cold read by 6.11 ns. The 11 µm trial still missed by 1.25 ns. At 8.5 µm the macro had 2.59 ns to spare.
  3. Measure delay inside the library cell. V3 replaced five RC paths with 52 DEL and 28 AND cells. A candidate without the QB capacitor had 13.51 ns schematic margin but completed six comparisons by 90 ns after extraction. At fixed slew and load, a DEL250 measured 280 ps in schematic, 283 ps with extracted devices, and 471 ps including internal wiring. The adopted controller used one COMMIT edge and registered per-bit delay taps.

With both SAR and CDAC extracted at worst interconnect, the SS-cold pairwise test read 8/8 frames with 6.95 ns margin. Stage 6 then added the other blocks, top-level wiring and the rail model.

Chip assembly · the pads and fill reopened the core

  1. From a 7 × 7 to a 9 × 9 pad ring. The supply rules drove a 36-pad arrangement. The drawn analog decoupling was 92.9 pF against 114.2 pF planned. Rechecking with extracted feeds gave 20.3 mV worst modeled droop against a 45 mV limit.
  2. The VIN pads forced a sampler revision. Flat latch-up checks flagged both samplers once VIN reached the pads. Utility fill also drove bootstrap plate-to-ground capacitance to roughly 66–127 fF. R2 revised guard rings, well spacing and local fill. The plates measured 37.9/40.4 fF and the two latch-up checks cleared. The final SS-cold core gate screen reached 99.1 mV against 100 mV.
  3. R3 through r8 resolved different periphery problems. R3 returned the analog clamp to its trunk, r4 distributed the ESD clamp feed and r5 reinforced AVSS pad landings on M1. R6 added comparator matching markers with a recorded offset risk. R7 reduced the square seal ring from 1 mm to 892 µm. R8 changed the clamp type and power cuts. The shuttle provider still needs to confirm the die outline.
  4. Fill7 closed density; waiver review continues. Fill6 excluded the IO cells, leaving 228 density markers in the IO band and corners. Fill7 allowed fill in the relevant IO areas and added M9 stripes around the ring. On the same r8 base, density markers fell from 2,672 to zero and total DRC results from 2,896 to 224. LVS matched, ERC counted 296 and flat latch-up counted zero. IO/ESD waivers and 15 dummy vias inside the core exclusion still need disposition.

Lessons and next steps

The most useful comparisons held the stimulus fixed. Ten identical inputs exposed the comparator speed/kickback trade-off, fixed slew and load isolated cell wiring delay, and the same r8 stream isolated the effect of fill. Reusing those controls will show which margins a change actually improves.

  1. Resolve two measured excursions. At FF cold, 16 VDS pairs exceeded the duration rule, with a worst case of 757.8 ps against 100 ps. At FF hot, the sampler reached 4.130 mV single-ended hold error against 3.516 mV. Recheck the worst register and the sampling edge, including timing and differential-error consequences.
  2. Complete performance characterization. Complete the 1,024-point noisy FFT and full 256-code scan. Report SNDR, ENOB, DNL and INL, then translate the extracted comparator offset σ of 12.05 mV over 200 seeds into off-chip correction range and input headroom.
  3. Close physical and package handoff. Review the IO/ESD waivers and exclusion-zone vias, and reconcile final VIN/VREF parasitics. Confirm the 892 µm outline, the 36-pad bond map and board reference and clock conditions.
