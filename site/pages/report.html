How a SAR ADC was designed and reworked

8-bit asynchronous SAR ADC · design review

The extracted ADC passed all 12 final corner runs and every DRC result on the filled chip has a class. Of the 36 reworks and open findings mapped below, 28 trace to a block verdict that did not survive the next layer’s load, interface or rule.

Several costly revisions began before layout. Two blocks held the same reference state, RC delays survived a digital-cell migration, and qualified blocks met their real pads only at integration. The circuit revisions, extractions and rule checks show where an earlier decision could have saved a rebuild.

Four rework chains

In October a simpler handshake produced all eight bits in the controller bench. In November the read deadline resurfaced in the RC macro, inside the library delay cell and at the board output. Each step added a load, clock path or observation point that the previous test did not exercise. Each chain below states the assumption, what broke it, the cost and the lesson; the dated records are folded underneath.

1

27 October–24 November · SAR timing

From RC to library delays to per-bit single-edge timing

  1. Assumption

    RC delay networks carry from the schematic to layout. The standard-cell migration kept them and its review covered function and FFT.

  2. What broke it

    Routed load. The QUIET resistor missed the SS-cold read by 6.11 ns; cell wiring took DEL250 from 280 to 471 ps; a correct code hid −0.393 LSB on bit 6.

  3. Cost

    Three timing schemes in four days, from a 2,058 µm² RC macro to a single-edge SAR with 6.95 ns of routed margin. FF-cold terminal stress reopened and is still open.

  4. Lesson

    Budget each delay under routed load at the fast and slow corner, and judge each decision by its analog value, not the code.

Dated record · 8 steps, 2 figures
  1. 27 October–4 November · Map the gates, carry the RC delays forward.

    The team reapplied the gate changes to the frozen L90/Mmain2 parent, isolating them from stress-diagnostic variants. The migration mapped inverters, NANDs, resettable DFFs, clock buffers and reference drive. Its contract explicitly retained RC/clear networks, the external SR latch load and reference resistors. Frozen B gained full-code and FFT regression coverage. Physical area and routed read time for the RC paths lay outside that review. Layout later measured about 845 µm² for the timing macro’s 231 MOM capacitors and ten resistors alone.

    Migration contract ↗ · Frozen B ↗ · Measured area ↗

  2. Nov 18 · The cost of RC timing

    The routed SAR macro occupied 2,058 µm². Its 231 MOM capacitors used 513 µm² and ten resistors another 333 µm². The 17.13 µm QUIET resistor passed in schematic and missed the SS-cold read by 6.11 ns after routing. An 11 µm trial still missed by 1.25 ns. An 8.5 µm resistor finally left 2.59 ns. A useful freeze criterion would have budgeted drawn area and delay under routed load.

    Area and resistor trials ↗

  3. Nov 19 · Standard delays passed a schematic stress screen

    V3 replaced five RC paths with 52 DEL and 28 AND cells. Four of six words were wrong on the first trial. Receiver buffers and final-tap request gating restored all 18 words at each of five corners. In the FF-cold two-frame thin-oxide scan, failing pairs fell from two to zero and worst over-limit duration from 457.8 to 75.0 ps against 100 ps. The result qualified that netlist and those waveforms. Routing requires another scan.

    Five corners and two-frame SOA ↗

  4. Nov 19 · Internal cell wiring changed the deadline

    Removing the QB capacitor still left 13.51 ns of SS-cold schematic margin on the nine-stage candidate. After coupled extraction it completed six comparisons by 90 ns in frame zero. Restoring the capacitor still gave six. At fixed slew and load, one DEL250 took 280 ps in schematic, 283 ps with device geometry alone and 471 ps including cell wiring. A later single-edge high tap measured 1.431 ns at FF-cold/CBEST_T and 3.894 ns at SS-cold/CWORST_T. Both fast-corner settling and slow-corner readout needed a budget.

    Capacitor restoration control ↗ · Paired tap delays ↗

  5. Nov 19–20 · Recovering read margin exposed late preset

    With a 40 ns sample pulse, shortening QUIET and sample enable let a dual-extracted DEL200 candidate read all four words. Yet its weighted error over 510 bottom plates reached 25.23 LSB at track opening in SS cold against 0.25 LSB. The team paused delay sweeps to review frame boundaries. The new single COMMIT edge stored the decision and advanced the trial, with preset on completion and the 29-stage idle guard removed. Under matched four-frame conditions, margin from last update to read rose from 3.642 to 10.917 ns.

    Track-opening error and stop decision ↗ · Matched replay ↗

  6. Nov 20 · A correct code hid the bit-6 reference error

    The initial 8/6 taps were checked against the instantaneous reference pins at all 510 bottom plates. A long 24/24-tap reference exposed −0.393 LSB of bit-6 TOP error at FF cold, including −0.355 LSB from reference recovery. A longer 9/6 tap then produced three bit-3 threshold crossings. Registering the select one commit earlier reduced them to one on the same 63→64 replay. Qualification now examined TOP, the reference pins and handoff edges at each decision.

    Long-window decomposition ↗ · Registered-select replay ↗

  7. Nov 20–21 · A 45 ns acquisition window and a bitwise budget

    Early single-edge trials still used 40/60 ns. Adoption moved the falling edge 5 ns later, allocating 45 ns to tracking and 55 ns to conversion while retaining the 90 ns read. Cell LPE and extracted-CDAC settling set the bit-6 through bit-0 taps to 11/9/7/6/5/4/4. SS-cold worst-interconnect margin was 7.59 ns with ideal SAR inter-cell wires. The routed SAR and extracted CDAC then passed 8/8 frames with 6.95 ns. Duty cycle, actual gate edges, per-bit waits and the external read aperture belong in a single timing budget.

    40 ns trial ↗ · 45/55 ns adoption ↗ · Routed pair ↗

  8. Nov 20 → 24 · Wider extraction reopened terminal stress

    Five FF-cold violations appeared before single-edge adoption. Changing the write-AND pin assignment and the select/reference logic brought the adopted 1,902-device scan to zero, with numerical resolution still under review. On the fill5 core with the supply/package network, the FF-cold extreme case found 16 over-duration VDS pairs, worst 757.8 ps. The netlists, loads, stimuli and scan scopes differ. Isolating the cause needs a matched replay at each integration boundary.

2

August–24 November · Sampler

One Bootstrap, reopened at every new layer

  1. Assumption

    The Bootstrap passed as a block: 0.126 mV hold error with a 1 µm switch on the flat 8-bit array.

  2. What broke it

    The extracted CDAC left 0.351 LSB at sample close, the VIN pad brought latch-up rules, and utility fill raised the boot-cap plates from 27–33 to 127 fF.

  3. Cost

    Four fingers, then r2 with wider guard rings and its own fill, requalified standalone and closed loop. SS cold now leaves about 1 mV on the gate screen while FF hot is 0.61 mV over its hold screen.

  4. Lesson

    Qualify the sampler with its pad, its fill and the real CDAC before freezing it, and track both corner margins together.

Dated record · 6 steps
  1. Late August–19 October · Replace the Bootstrap topology.

    Sizing, discharge and reset-order experiments left both hold error and core-device stress. The designer’s dated notes identify the replacement topology they supplied. It used twelve T28 1.8 V MOS devices, a 1 pF bootstrap capacitor and a local inverter. On the same 10-bit split CDAC, worst differential hold error across five TT inputs fell from 1.536 to 0.394 mV. The 8-bit flat array increased the load. The 0.5 µm switch then gave 12.59 mV error; increasing it to 1 µm brought that down to 0.126 mV. Each pass belonged to a particular load and corner.

    Designer’s notes ↗ · Same-load comparison ↗ · 8-bit retest ↗

  2. 21 November · The extracted ADC reopened sampler sizing.

    On 21 November the extracted ADC reopened sampler sizing. The two-finger switch missed the 0.25 LSB screen on seven of ten SS-cold frames; removing well-proximity effects still left 1.52 LSB. Four fingers halved the time constant from 4.6 to 2.3 ns. Six failed the standalone FF-cold error limit. Four were adopted. Pad connectivity and fill would reopen this block again. Sizing and retests ↗

  3. Nov 22–23 · Connecting VIN to a pad reopened the frozen Bootstrap.

    The latch-up rules depended on pad connectivity. Hierarchical DRC reported only the negative side. A flat run found one plus six markers on each sampler. R2 widened the guard rings and moved the PMOS row to give 2.40 µm VIN-to-PMOS diffusion spacing against 2.30 µm required. The first widening ran into dummy poly and had to turn outward. Clearing latch-up reopened fill, extraction, standalone sampling and closed-loop qualification.

    Flat diagnosis ↗ · R2 requalification ↗

  4. Nov 22 · Utility fill loaded the boot capacitor.

    The boot capacitor is 200 MOM units over 444 of the cell’s 613 µm². Unfilled, each plate measured 27–33 fF to ground. Top-level M1–M9 fill raised that to 127 fF; OD/PO-only fill still gave 66–69 fF. The surroundings added under 2 fF, so the dummy shapes inside the cell were the cause. The Bootstrap now owns its fill pattern, extracted at 37.9 / 40.4 fF standalone and 38–42 fF in the core. At SS cold the filled r2 raised the sample-close residual to 0.093–0.097 LSB, from 0.039 LSB unfilled.

    Fill variants and plate loads ↗ · Filled r2 parasitics and residual ↗

  5. Nov 23 · About 1 mV left on the SS-cold gate screen.

    The SS-cold Bootstrap gate screen has a 0.100 V limit. On the early routed clock the old core read 0.118 V. The filled r2 read 0.0972 V on the same deck and passed without its written fallback. The final fill5-core runs read 0.0991 V. The early routed run had already shown a slow Bootstrap gate fall.

    Routed gate screen ↗ · Gate-screen decision ↗ · Final-run summary ↗ · Final corner suite ↗

  6. Nov 24 · FF hot squeezes the same sampler from the other side.

    At FF hot the final fill5-core netlist reached 4.130 mV single-ended hold error against the 3.516 mV standalone screen (half an LSB). The carry case passed at 3.180 mV. The status record on main lists this result as open for the Bootstrap owner. The sampler therefore sits about 1 mV inside its SS-cold gate limit and 0.61 mV outside its FF-hot hold limit.

3

21–24 November · from core to package boundary

Pad ring, clamps and seal ring: rules read after the blocks froze

  1. Assumption

    The pad ring, cuts, clamps and seal ring could follow the core, and markers inside library cells were waivable.

  2. What broke it

    The library supply rule, a generator that doubled each cut, the clamp placement and ESD current-density rules, a clamp drawn with the wrong gate direction and the seal-ring corner rule.

  3. Cost

    Chip views r2 to r8, a 36-pad ring instead of 7 × 7, and fill redone for an 892 µm die. Ten clamp markers rode through several fill passes.

  4. Lesson

    Cite the rule text behind every exception, and count cuts, check clamp masters and fix the outline before fill.

Dated record · 6 steps
  1. Nov 21–22 · Blocks froze before pad supplies were settled.

    The first 7 × 7 plan had one analog PVDD3AC where the library required at least three, and omitted PVSS2AC. Checking supplies, corner cells and domain boundaries yielded a minimum of 34 pads. The 36-pad replacement changed placement, reference routing and the rail budget.

    Pad-rule audit ↗ · Revised rail budget ↗

  2. Nov 22 · Slew-limited data pads for simultaneous switching.

    D0–D7 moved from PDDW16SDGZ (16 mA, no slew control) to PRUW08SDGZ (8 mA, slew-limited), with the same pins and footprint. With the databook driving factors the IO cells sum to 1.547 instead of 3.84, so two VDDIO and two VSSIO cells meet the TSMC IO application-note rule. SAMPLE stays on PDDW16SDGZ.

    Data-pad change ↗ · IO ring and switching budget ↗

  3. Nov 22 · The IO generator inserted an extra cut at each domain boundary.

    The plan called for one PRCUTA_G per boundary. The generator filled each 40 µm gap with two. Renaming supplies reduced ring ERC from 334 findings to six. The two floating wells and four floating substrates lay exactly at the abutting cuts. The ring review accepted the group because the findings were inside library cells. R8 replaced one cut at each boundary with PFILLER20_G. Neither cell contains devices, so the LVS netlist was insensitive to the mistake.

    Original ring disposition ↗ · R8 correction ↗

  4. Nov 22–23 · Decoupling and clamp protection competed for the same space.

    The decap estimate omitted the clamp footprint. Moving the analog clamp to make room violated its required placement between the branch pads and core. R3 restored the clamp, added MOM to reach 92.9 pF and rechecked 20.3 mV worst droop with extracted feeds. A separate review applied the 1 Ω inter-IO bus rule to the pad-to-clamp path. The corrected 1.3 A current-density check drove r4 feed straps and r5 AVSS pad landings.

    Clamp return ↗ · Rule correction ↗ · Landing fix ↗ · Rail recheck ↗

  5. Nov 23 · Known PCLAMPC markers survived several fill passes.

    The initial chip assembly had already localized ten gate-rule results to PCLAMPC_H_G. They were listed for written waiver review. A cell-swap experiment showed that H_G at R0 had the wrong gate direction. R8 used V_G with the same footprint and pins, clearing all ten. Marker location established where to investigate. Orientation, library revision and the release notes determined the appropriate disposition.

    Contemporaneous waiver list ↗ · Swap trial ↗ · Rule disposition ↗

  6. Nov 23 · The sample seal ring preceded the corner-rule audit.

    R3 through r6 used the lab's 1 mm sample ring, which defined the exclusions for fill revisions 1–5. Later the team reconstructed its tiling and probed CSR.R.1. The deck creates the relevant triangles with the seal ring absent, so a separate marked die-corner stream was needed. The IO corners implied an 882 µm minimum. R7 chose 892 µm with 10 µm clearance, saving about 20% of area and forcing new fill boundaries. Shuttle confirmation remains open.

4

August–24 November · verification scope

Checks that passed what they did not test

  1. Assumption

    A passing check covered the interface it was run on.

  2. What broke it

    An ideal reference controller, partial LVS without the output register, hierarchical latch-up DRC, a cell-deck comparator DRC that never runs the matching check, a supply-name ERC probe and a delay element that removed its receivers’ loads.

  3. Cost

    An output register added at integration, the r2 sampler, a ring correction in r8, route budgets rebuilt on RC ladders and a matching-class deviation accepted on the frozen comparator.

  4. Lesson

    State each verdict’s scope, then replay the real periphery against the core. That replay is why core-only Stage 6 results carry to the chip.

Dated record · 6 steps
  1. August–October · Front-end convergence and its physical assumptions.

    The 10-bit behavioral model first tested the noise allocation in August. September experiments isolated bootstrap turn-off paths under CDAC load. October’s flat 8-bit array removed the split-capacitor bridge ratio, but the choice of unit capacitance changed the sampling load and required a new switch test. All 16,384 ideal 8-bit model outputs matched Python code for code. Injecting the allocated noise reduced ENOB from 7.80 to 7.33 bits. With 500 Ω at the reference and 1,800 Ω at the bottom plates, the macro-model could still return the correct code with 4.41 LSB of settling error. The need to check analog values at each decision was visible before layout. 8-bit migration ↗

  2. 20 October · Simplify the SAR before drawing it.

    The custom CMOS controller acknowledged decisions and resets, handled timeouts and retained a separate output bank. At slow-hot it finished seven bits by the deadline. SAMPLE then initialized a local bit loop. Removing the extra bank and EOC reduced the expanded MOS count from 1,742 to 1,476. Thirteen functional and timing tests passed with ideal analog interfaces. The record already warned that a stalled comparison could leave a partial code. Chip integration still had to assign an output capture edge and pad drive.

    Controller and test boundary ↗

  3. 20–23 October · Reference control crossed an ideal interface.

    R1 closed the loop using physical reference switches and ideal gate control. In R3 a frame completed eight comparisons yet returned 1 instead of 65. Replaying the same input history isolated a rejected timer callback in the ideal controller; the fix restored code 65. R4 then implemented native reference control, followed by an experiment moving staged reset into idle. The contemporary notes flagged the handoff gap between the digital and analog work.

    Frame 9 replay ↗ · Native reference control ↗ · Idle-reset comparison ↗

  4. Oct 20–Nov 22 · Who holds the previous conversion?

    The October SAR simplification removed the output bank. Sampling cleared the working word, so a correct core read did not establish a held board output. The first IO plan reserved a register outside the core, but the partial assembly passed its scoped LVS while the register was still undrawn. Eight DVDD flip-flops eventually captured on the same SAMPLE edge that cleared the core. Adding DEL250 on D raised FF-hot hold slack from 0.30 to 0.50 ns.

    Working-word interface ↗ · First floorplan ↗ · Partial assembly ↗ · Capture timing ↗

  5. Nov 22 · An ideal delay element dropped the receiver loads.

    The first Stage 2 route-budget checks inserted a Verilog-A delay on each handshake crossing. It draws no input current, so it removed the comparator, SAR clock and CODE receivers from their drivers: with 200 ps added, SS-cold margin came out 7.84 ns, above the 7.77 ns control. Those runs were kept as diagnostics. The budgets were redone with four-segment RC ladders between the real drivers and receivers: 11.4 fF per comparator output left 4.16 ns at SS cold and twice the planned length left 0.66 ns. A later correction found that every deck still carried the undrawn model loads of about 10 fF, so the adopted load limits keep about 10 fF of headroom.

    Stage 2 route checks ↗ · Budget adoption and load correction ↗

  6. Nov 23 · The periphery replay ties the core verdicts to the chip.

    Stage 6 ran the extracted fill5 core with ideal VIN, the Stage 1 package and reference model and no output path, then replayed the periphery against those same runs. The 56.6 Ω VIN routes and pad capacitance raised the SS-cold residual from 0.1015 to 0.1085 LSB (+0.007 LSB) with identical codes and the same 8.73 ns read margin, so no widening was needed. At FF hot, adding the output register, pad drivers and 15 pF per pin kept the codes and moved read margin from 30.27 to 29.96 ns (−0.31 ns). Driver switching against sample close remains a separate analysis.

    Run identities and controls ↗ · Peripheral verdicts ↗

What the records show about review

The reference-driver issue identified duplicate state on 15 November. The floorplan issue called out scattered area and interface constraints on the 16th. The block audit catalogued missing XREF, top-level and standalone checks on the 21st. Several risks had already been raised. The handoff failed to carry each open condition to the next freeze. Partial LVS could correctly match a netlist without the output register. A name-dependent ERC probe could explain warnings without verifying the planned cut count. Both decisions needed a reviewer to check what was being accepted.

Comparator · 16–23 November · three-row layout and matching class

16–21 November · StrongARM layout

Comparator · three shared-diffusion rows

The early sym2 StrongARM occupied 7.96 × 12.46 µm. Packing it into three rows exposed terminal-mapping and unequal finger-width problems. V7 used individual 0.5 µm fingers, explicit schematic connectivity, shared diffusion and input escapes between rows. The footprint fell from 99.18 to 5.74 µm².

V3/v4 grouped transistors into multifinger PCells. The gate assignments and source/drain names became difficult to reconcile with the circuit. V6 restored one instance per schematic device but introduced a 0.5/1 µm diffusion step and an extra tail row. V7 reconciled compact placement with traceable terminals.

On the same ten TT points, maximum decision delay fell from 517.3 to 301.7 ps, while peak differential kickback rose from 52.18 to 59.63 µV. V8 added M2 supply pins and redundant vias. Tail-to-ground resistance fell from 38.7 to 29.4 Ω and maximum delay was 314.3 ps. An extra clock via on one side caused a wrong decision at +0.1 mV, so the symmetric version was retained.

Geometry, parasitics and delay ↗ · V8 qualification ↗

  1. Nov 23 · The compact comparator could not meet the high-matching class.

    With ANARRAY_H markers over the comparator arrays, AN.R.79.2m failed on OD density: the array-to-halo difference was 0.348 (N) and 0.257 (P) against 0.10. Rebuilding the arrays with identical dummies was rejected. The comparator got normal MATCHING markers instead, which clear MATCH.WARN.1 (now 0), and the deviation from high matching was accepted as a design decision. MATCH.WARN.1 states that matching may degrade by at least 3× when the high or medium array rules are not met, and the rule gives no upper bound. Scaling the 200-seed extracted sample extremes (−34.7 and +34.1 mV, σ 12.05 mV) by three gives about ±104 mV. The record calls this a sensitivity example only, not a worst case. For scale, 104 mV is 11.6 % of the 0.90 V one-sided full scale, and a −1 dBFS sine leaves about 98 mV of headroom per side, so a die with that offset would just clip. The ADC is uncalibrated; correction range, resolution and qualification remain open in the specification and the integration tracker.

  1. 24–29 October · The worst stress moved between devices.

    At FF cold, quartering the series gate resistance improved plate settling from 2.217 to 2.166 ns but deepened undershoot from −56.94 to −75.61 mV.   4. 16–18 November · Shortening TOP routes exposed a small-bit weight error.

    Opening and restoring B1 in a unit MOM / 1:2 pilot verified that LVS detected the fault. The first full array then required 16.736 mm of functional routing and 404.44 µm of TOP per side. On 18 November facing adjacent TOP rows and folding 65 switch pairs into 16 per side cut the differential footprint from 8,354.62 to 3,738.62 µm². Each TOP route fell to 213.27 µm. In matched TT prefill fixtures the sum of bit weights rose from 95.292% to 96.994% of schematic, but the worst gain-normalized bit residual grew from 0.049 to 0.129 LSB.

  5. 18–21 November · Fill and long trunks overweighted the small bits.

    Utility fill raised the fixed TOP load from about 38.6 to about 141 fF per side. With array-owned fill, conductor-level extraction then located a second error. Coupling inside each MOM unit was uniform, but long M4 trunks overweighted the small bits, giving −0.35 LSB DNL at transitions such as 47→48. Reordering just the central bits by weight produced standalone transient DNL/INL of 0.123/0.084 LSB with 4.22% gain error. All ten codes matched in the integrated SS-cold run, yet one frame exceeded the 0.25 LSB sampling limit at 0.351 LSB. That result sent the issue back to the sampler.

  6. Nov 22 · The first on-chip reference resistor failed the rail budget.

    The first RREF used 42 poly segments per rail with thin trunk stacks. Extracted from pin to rail end it measured 21.7 / 24.6 Ω, and the rail budget gave 0.12 LSB of decision error at 22 Ω against the 0.10 LSB limit. Each rail was rebuilt with 52 segments, 2 × 2 cut stacks and strapped risers: 17.2 Ω with 6.23 pF of CREF, and a worst decision error of 0.035 LSB at FF cold across the tolerance box.

Fill, budgets and the sign-off ledger · 22–24 November
  1. Nov 22 · Static leakage separated from switching charge.

    At FF hot (0.99 V, 125 °C) static leakage is a large part of the measured charge. A recorded decision reports it separately, from a quiet window in every frame: 227 µA on VDD (225 µW) and 7.9 µA on each reference. Scored on switching charge, the reference budgets pass. Supply switching charge at 1.01–1.09× its budget was accepted, because these budgets are decap-sizing proxies and the checks they protect pass: supply droop 28 mV against 45 mV, supply pin peak 1.70 mA against 5.5 mA and reference pin peak 4.22 mA against 5.2 mA. No limit changed. In the final fill5-core runs the carry case reached 1.11× with 22 mV droop.
